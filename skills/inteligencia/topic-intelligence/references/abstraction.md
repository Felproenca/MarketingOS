# Abstraction Protocol

For every evidence item, extract:

1. `surface`: exact topic and format;
2. `frame`: how the problem is interpreted;
3. `tension`: fear, desire, status conflict or unresolved question;
4. `mechanism`: explanation offered for why it happens;
5. `proof`: data, demonstration, authority, story or assertion;
6. `platform_grammar`: why the expression fits that channel;
7. `response`: what the audience asks, contests, saves or repeats.

Then compare:

- universal frames appearing across languages;
- local frames unique to a market;
- ideas moving from one language into another;
- claims repeated without proof;
- saturated hooks;
- contradictions between expert and audience language;
- useful ideas that have not yet received strong Portuguese expression.

Do not translate the best-performing sentence. Translate the underlying tension
and rebuild it in the brand's own language.

The final thesis must use this structure:

```text
People currently describe [problem] as [dominant frame].
Evidence across [channels/languages] suggests the deeper tension is [tension].
Most content misses [whitespace or contradiction].
This brand can credibly argue [editorial thesis]
because [brand proof or lived authority].
The acquisition job is [observable next action].
```
